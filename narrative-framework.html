The Cryptomath Narrative Framework

A practical way to judge crypto narratives before they become obvious.

Quick answer

The Narrative Framework evaluates a crypto narrative across seven dimensions.

Problem pressure, Timing, Liquidity path, Attention velocity, Product proof, Distribution surface, and Asymmetry.

It is not a prediction machine. It structures a thesis, compares signals, and helps distinguish conviction from late consensus.

A crypto narrative is a shared thesis about how a technology, use case, or market change will create value. It becomes observable when independent actors begin to use the same language, build products, and direct capital toward the theme.

The total score measures how robust a narrative is as a research topic. It measures neither price potential nor earliness.

A strong narrative is not just a story people repeat. It is a story with pressure behind it, capital moving toward it, and enough proof to survive first contact with reality.

The seven dimensions

Each dimension is scored from 0 to 5. The total sets a research priority.

1

Problem pressure

The narrative must answer a real frustration, structural shift, or new demand. The stronger the pressure, the less the narrative needs pure hype.

0 to 5Need
  • 0: No clearly identifiable problem. A solution looking for a problem.
  • 3: A real, documented problem, but still confined to a niche or avoidable.
  • 5: A structural, costly, recurring problem observed beyond a single crypto project, with concrete demand.
2

Timing

A good idea can still be too early or too late. Timing checks whether the market, infrastructure, regulation, and user behavior are aligned now.

0 to 5Window
  • 0: An essential condition is still missing, with no near-term path in sight.
  • 3: Several conditions are in place, but an important dependency or trigger is still missing.
  • 5: Essential conditions allow adoption now, with at least one concrete catalyst identifiable within 6 months. Regulation counts here only if relevant to the narrative.
3

Liquidity path

Narratives need a path for capital to enter. Tokens, listings, incentives, yields, unlocks, and liquid venues all matter.

0 to 5Capital
  • 0: No liquid asset or proxy genuinely exposed to the narrative.
  • 3: At least one liquid proxy exists, but its exposure is imperfect or its access and liquidity remain limited.
  • 5: One or more liquid, accessible proxies provide sufficiently direct and deep exposure to track the thesis.
4

Attention velocity

Attention is not just follower count. It is speed, repetition, memetic clarity, and how quickly independent groups start using the same language.

0 to 5Mindshare
  • 0: Attention is flat, declining, or mainly sustained by the project itself.
  • 3: Visible acceleration in specialist communities. Shared language is beginning to emerge.
  • 5: Measurable acceleration from a still-low base, echoed by several independent groups over a defined recent period.
5

Product proof

The best narratives still need evidence. Usage, revenue, retention, developer activity, user behavior, or credible prototypes reduce narrative risk.

0 to 5Reality
  • 0: A concept, announcement, or whitepaper. No real usage.
  • 3: A usable product with real users, but usage is still low, concentrated, or heavily supported by incentives.
  • 5: Recurring usage observed over multiple periods. Fees, revenue, retention, or developer activity show demand that does not mainly depend on incentives.
6

Distribution surface

A narrative travels faster when founders, exchanges, apps, creators, communities, funds, and users all have reasons to spread it.

0 to 5Spread
  • 0: The project and its investors are almost the only parties with an interest in spreading the topic.
  • 3: Several independent channels exist, but distribution remains concentrated.
  • 5: Several independent surfaces each have their own reason to spread the narrative, such as apps, infrastructure, exchanges, developers, creators, funds, and users.
7

Asymmetry

The final question is simple. Is meaningful potential still left relative to how obvious, crowded, and priced-in the narrative already is?

0 to 5Valuation
  • 0: The favorable scenario already appears largely reflected in the valuations of exposed assets.
  • 3: Some potential remains, but significant repricing has already occurred.
  • 5: The thesis could still have a substantial impact relative to current valuations, and most relevant proxies have not yet reacted strongly.

Saturation

Saturation measures how far attention has already spread. It is not included in the total score.

Low: the topic remains mostly specialized, its category is not yet established, repricing remains limited, and AI models rarely or never cite it when asked about current narratives.

Medium: the narrative is clearly recognized by the crypto ecosystem, receives regular coverage, and several assets or projects are already associated with the theme.

High: the narrative has become an obvious category, is ubiquitous in crypto or institutional coverage, is associated with assets that have already reacted, and is cited spontaneously by several AI models.

AI model responses are one indicator among others of a narrative’s spread, never the sole criterion for saturation. Each measurement is dated; comparisons over time require the recorded engine, version and search conditions described below.

Saturation protocol v1

Locked September 29, 2026. Saturation is assessed for the broad narrative category A. Sub-thesis B is recorded separately in Media and AI, but does not enter the Low / Medium / High classification. The saturated reference is the median of two fixed narratives, prediction markets and AI crypto, measured on the same day by the same method.

  1. Category (axis 1). Check CoinGecko, CoinMarketCap, DefiLlama, Artemis, Messari and Kaito. At least 4 of the 6 taxonomies must be measurable; a generic category such as Payments does not count, and an inaccessible source is not measured. Low: ≤25% of measurable taxonomies; Medium: >25% and <2/3; High: ≥2/3.
  2. Media (axis 2, last 30 days). Search CoinDesk, The Block, Cointelegraph, Decrypt, Blockworks, Cryptoast, Journal du Coin and Cointribune. Count an article only when narrative A is its main subject; count republications or syndication of the same article once. Compare the count with the saturated reference: Low <20%; Medium 20–60%; High >60%. Record sub-thesis B separately.
  3. Google Trends (axis 3). Use the 12-month weekly view, English terms, worldwide. Compare the candidate term with the reference terms in the same query. The 90-day average uses the last 13 weeks. A sustained rise requires both a last-28-day average at least 50% above the preceding 28-day average and at least 3 of the last 4 weeks above the mean of the preceding 4 weeks. A single spike remains an observation, not sufficient evidence.
  4. Social attention (axis 4). Not measured until a stable, reproducible metric is available. No social metric is inferred.
  5. Repricing (axis 5, 90 days). Fix the token basket in the candidate sheet before collection. Use ETH and the candidate-specific CoinGecko category as benchmarks; use CoinGecko prices at day J and J−90. For each token, A = token 90-day performance − ETH 90-day performance; B = token 90-day performance − category 90-day performance. Token relative score = median(A, B); axis score = median of the basket’s token relative scores. Low <20 points; Medium 20–60 points; High >60 points. This is a secondary signal.
  6. Spontaneous AI mentions (axis 6). Run the three fixed prompts/queries across the five T0 surfaces: ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini and Google AI Overviews. For conversational engines, use a fresh temporary session without prior context. For Google AI Overviews, use classic Google Search. Do not substitute AI Mode. The prompts, unchanged in every run, are:
    1. Quels sont les narratifs crypto émergents en ce moment ?
    2. Quelles tendances crypto commencent à apparaître mais ne sont pas encore mainstream ?
    3. What crypto narratives are gaining traction right now?

    Recorded T0 reference settings (September 28, 2026; modes, not model version numbers):

    • ChatGPT: signed out / anonymized session; Medium mode; web search enabled.
    • Perplexity: mode unknown; web search enabled; account and interface country not recorded.
    • Claude: Medium mode; web search enabled; account and interface country not recorded.
    • Gemini: Flash mode; web search enabled; account and interface country not recorded.
    • Google AI Overviews: classic Google Search with AI Overview; google.fr / France; Chrome; web search enabled; logged-in Google account distinct from The Cryptomath; not AI Mode.

    Measure the share of valid responses that spontaneously mention narrative A. A generic answer is insufficient; a project cited for another reason does not count. Candidate-specific coding rules are locked before collection; record sub-thesis B separately. If an engine is unavailable, use the valid responses as the denominator. Low <20%; Medium 20–50%; High >50% and a mention by at least 3 engines.

Collection and interpretation. Measure all axes of a run on the same day. Retain exact queries, captures and observations. Before collecting, lock the candidate sheet: definition of narrative A, sub-thesis B, Media inclusion rules, Google Trends terms, repricing basket, candidate-specific CoinGecko reference category and AI coding rules. Choose none of these after seeing results; do not reweight an axis after reading results. Date and document any later protocol change; do not silently revise past classifications. Classification is Indeterminate when fewer than 5 axes are measurable; Low when at least 4 axes are Low and none is High; High when at least 3 axes are High; Medium in every other case.

AI measurement log

For every AI run, retain the collection date; engine/product; displayed model or version when identifiable, otherwise explicitly “version not exposed”; web search/browsing state (enabled, disabled or not applicable); language; exact prompt; session or search condition (fresh temporary session without prior context for conversational engines; classic Google Search for Google AI Overviews); complete response; screenshot; and coding (mention yes/no, with a justification if ambiguous).

An AI component comparison over time is called comparable only when the necessary parameters were recorded. Flag any engine-version change or difference in search conditions in the record. Earlier observations missing engine, version or search metadata are not retroactively treated as fully reproducible; do not invent historical version numbers. The first measurement using this complete log is the first fully reproducible baseline under this documentation.

Documentation clarification — October 10, 2026. No scoring threshold or past classification was changed. Earlier AI observations lacking complete engine/version/search metadata are not retroactively treated as fully reproducible.

Each analysis publishes the observations that justify its saturation level.

Attention velocity measures how quickly attention is rising. Saturation measures how far it has already spread. Asymmetry concerns what is already reflected in valuations.

Scoring bands

The score guides research priority without replacing judgment.

0-10

Noise

Signal is too weak, too vague, or mainly promotional.

11-20

Watchlist

Interesting elements are emerging, but the evidence, timing, or structure remain insufficient.

21-28

Active

The narrative is sufficiently supported to warrant in-depth research and regular monitoring.

29-35

Strong

Strong convergence across observed dimensions; a research priority.

Strong implies neither high upside potential nor an opportunity to buy.

Early Detection Zone: a total score of at least 21 and Low saturation. The narrative is sufficiently supported to qualify as Active, yet remains lightly disseminated. Between 18 and 20 with Low saturation, it stays on the watchlist and is not presented as a detection.

Analyses published before September 28, 2026 retain their original methodology and are not retrospectively rescored.

How to apply it

Use the same grid every time. That is what makes the framework useful.

StepQuestionWhat to look for
1What problem creates pressure?A real pain point, market inefficiency, regulatory shift, cultural shift, or user behavior change.
2Why now?New infrastructure, new liquidity, better UX, fresh incentives, or a catalyst that did not exist before.
3Where can capital enter?Liquid tokens, upcoming launches, public markets, points, yield, funds, or exchange attention.
4Who repeats the story?Builders, users, analysts, funds, exchanges, communities, and creators using similar language independently.
5What proves it is real?Usage, revenue, retention, developer traction, public data, or repeated behavior across more than one project.
6How far can it spread?Simple memes, clear categories, easy onboarding, strong communities, and incentives for many actors to talk about it.
7Is there still asymmetry?Compare upside with consensus, valuation, crowding, unlocks, reflexivity, and downside risk.

Common traps

Most mistakes come from confusing attention with signal.

Late consensus

When everyone can explain the narrative perfectly, most of the discovery may already have happened.

Fake usage

Incentives can create activity that disappears as soon as rewards stop. Usage needs context.

No liquidity path

A strong idea with no practical path for capital to enter can remain intellectually compelling but difficult to express through liquid markets.
